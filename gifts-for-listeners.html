Gifts for People Who'd Rather Listen

Last updated November 2026

How this page pays for itself: every link below goes to Amazon. As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases, at no extra cost to you. That is what keeps Read‑Aloud free.

People use Read‑Aloud to hear their own writing, get through a long chapter, practise a language, or fall asleep to an article. If someone on your list is like that, here is a short list of things that make listening and reading easier. Nothing here is expensive for the sake of it, and each one is on the list for a plain reason.

For the student with a reading list

  • Noise‑cancelling headphones. Over‑ear wireless headphones that shut out a dorm, a library or a bus, so the chapter is the only thing they hear.
  • A bamboo book stand. Holds a textbook open with page clips, hands free, while they follow along or take notes.
  • An adjustable tablet stand. Lifts a tablet or phone to eye level, which is kinder to the neck during a long reading.

For the writer who proofreads by ear

  • Small wireless earbuds. For hearing a draft read back on a walk, where the clumsy sentences are easier to catch.
  • Glasses that filter blue light. For long days in front of a screen.

For the reader who finds small print hard work

  • A lighted magnifying glass. Handheld, with its own light, for labels, menus and the small print on paper.
  • An Echo Dot. A small speaker that reads the news, the weather and audiobooks aloud when asked.
  • Audible. Whole books read by professional narrators. See Amazon's page for the current offer and its terms.

For the bedtime listener

  • A sleep headband with speakers. A soft band with thin speakers inside, comfortable for listening on your side.
  • A reading light worn around the neck. Lights the page and not the room, so nobody else is woken up.
  • A sunrise alarm clock. Brightens slowly before the alarm, for the morning after a late chapter.

A gift that costs nothing

Show them Read‑Aloud. Paste in any text, pick a voice, and it reads itself back. It is free, with no account to set up. The how‑to guide covers the basics.

Prices and availability change, so check Amazon's page before you buy. We picked these ourselves; nobody paid to be on the list.
